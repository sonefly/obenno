About this site 梵音殿 OBENNO

A quiet reading room for Chinese Mahāyāna Buddhist texts — sources stated, rules written down, nothing to sign up for.

OBENNO (梵音殿, "the Hall of the Pure Voice") is a small, independent, non-commercial site for reading Chinese Buddhist scripture, with a few aids around it: a recitation player, a daily practice page, a glossary and two short essays. It is a static site — no accounts, no database, no advertising, nothing for sale, and no paywall.

The interface is Chinese-first, because the texts themselves are Chinese. English here is editorial metadata, not a translation of scripture: we do not machine-translate the canon, and we would rather leave a passage untranslated than offer a rendering nobody has checked.

The name

梵音殿 reads literally as "hall of the pure voice". The 梵音 (fàn yīn) in it is not Brahma the god and not a Sanskrit transliteration: in Chinese Buddhist usage it is the pure, harmonious quality of the Buddha's teaching voice.

OBENNO is the same place under a short sign — six letters, two syllables, no dictionary meaning of its own. It exists because Chinese characters cannot be typed or said on a non-Chinese keyboard, and because a six-letter word is easier to repeat over the phone and to search than a transliteration. It never stands alone: wherever it appears, the site writes 梵音殿 OBENNO as one lockup, so the meaning comes from the Chinese and the handle from OBENNO.

The 殿 (hall) is an image, not a claim: this is not a temple, not a branch of any temple, and not anyone's official site. It is one person's reading room, kept as a static site.

Texts and sources

The base text is the CBETA electronic edition of the Taishō Tripiṭaka (T numbers), with Chinese-authored works cited to the specific edition used. Where our handling differs from the base text, it is stated here rather than left implicit.

Base textCBETA Taishō Tripiṭaka (T numbers); title pages give fascicle and section counts
PunctuationFollows CBETA's modern punctuation and paragraphing; no new punctuation is invented and no sentence break is moved
OrthographyCharacters kept as in the base text; variant forms are not normalised, and rare glyphs fall back to system fonts rather than being replaced
PronunciationGiven in the traditional Chinese chant reading, not as Sanskrit transliteration; characters with divergent readings (佛, 阇, 伽, 般, 若, 啰, 咄) are corrected one by one
Critical apparatusOmitted: no variant readings are listed and no reading is silently altered. An evident error in the base text is left as printed and noted outside the text
Tables of contentsOnly two long works (中论 and 楞严经) carry fascicle/chapter indexes; anchors are inserted between paragraphs, with no re-segmentation and no added or removed characters (332 / 485 sections, as stated)

This is not a critical edition and does not replace a printed or scholarly one. For research and formal citation, use CBETA and the printed Taishō edition; where our punctuation differs from the current CBETA text, CBETA prevails.

Editorial rules

  • No rewriting — scripture is carried over and paragraphed, never paraphrased; explanatory matter lives on separate pages, not inside the text.
  • Traceable — each text page states title, fascicles, sections, basket and source, so a citation maps onto a CBETA volume and text number.
  • No promises — no claims of efficacy, no miracles or prophecy; the almanac is a date table, not an omen calendar.
  • No paywall — no subscription, no membership, no ads, nothing for sale. One voluntary merit box (Alipay, WeChat or international transfer) covers domain and hosting costs; it is not a ticket, has no counterpart, and reading is never conditional on it.
  • Correctable — errors of character, fascicle or punctuation are fixed on the page once verified, and the date of the correction is recorded.

Privacy in brief

No accounts and no personal data are collected. Reading preferences, practice counts and your last position in a text are kept in your own browser's local storage. Two disclosed counters exist: Microsoft Clarity for anonymous page analytics, and a self-hosted greeting counter on the home page that resolves a province/city, hashes the IP for same-day de-duplication and discards it after two days. Both are blockable without losing any function. Full detail is on the privacy page (Chinese).

Corrections and contact

Corrections are welcome at github.com/sonefly/obenno/issues or by email to hello@obenno.com — please give the page URL, the paragraph, and, for scripture, the CBETA volume and text number. The site is kept by one person as a private undertaking: there is no support desk and no guaranteed reply time, and no commercial partnership or paid placement is accepted.

Where to start

  • Diamond Sūtra · Amitābha Sūtra · Platform Sūtra — short, widely read, complete on one page each
  • The Canon — English index of the 29 full texts: title, basket, fascicles, sections, Taishō number and one line on each; links open the Chinese text
  • The Ground — twelve doors of doctrine, from the Three Jewels to calm-and-insight
  • History — from the Buddha's community to the modern period, with our base-text note
  • Glossary — 83 terms, and full-text search across the canon

Reader controls (type size, line width, pinyin toggle, single-line repeat, citation and print) are described on the Chinese pages; the labels are short and the icons self-explanatory.
