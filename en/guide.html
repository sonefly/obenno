How to read a text page Guide for English readers

The scripture here is Chinese and stays Chinese — no machine translation. Everything around it is in English, so you can find a text, follow its structure and cite it without reading the characters.

What is in English, and what is not

English is editorial, not scriptural. It names the text, counts it, locates it in the canon and tells you in one line what it is; it never stands in for a passage of scripture. A Chinese-only reader will find the same page to be the main text; an English-only reader will find the frame. Both are deliberate.

In EnglishTitle and English title, basket and fascicle counts, section numbers, Taishō number, one-line abstract, the canon index, this guide, the licence and privacy pages.
In ChineseThe scripture itself, its section headings, the glossary entries, the daily-practice and rites pages, the essays.
NeverNo machine translation of scripture, no paraphrase offered as if it were the text, no invented punctuation.

Section numbers are the site's own, not CBETA's page or line numbers. They exist so that a passage can be pointed to in one click; the Taishō number is what makes a citation traceable to the printed canon.

Two behaviours are not labelled. Clicking any paragraph copies its citation — Chinese title, section number, the opening characters and the URL — with a short toast to confirm. And if you leave a long text halfway, a chip appears on your next visit offering to resume where you stopped. Both run entirely in your browser.

Finding a text

  • By English title — the canon index lists all 34 texts with English title, Chinese title, basket, fascicles, sections and Taishō number.
  • By Taishō number — search accepts T08n0235 or diamond and returns the text itself; full-text search, however, matches Chinese characters only. English words will never match the body text, and the search page says so rather than pretending.
  • By Chinese word — a single character is enough: 空, 心, 度. Partial phrases work; there is no need to remember a whole sentence.
  • By term — the glossary panel in the hall page has a Chinese/English toggle for terms; the standalone glossary page is Chinese.

Citing a passage

The click-to-copy citation on each paragraph gives you the Chinese form. For English-language writing, cite the text by its Taishō identity and the section number, and link the page:

《金剛般若波羅蜜經》 (Diamond Sūtra), T08 no. 0235, sec. 5. 梵音殿 OBENNO, https://www.obenno.com/sutra/jg.html#s5 (accessed 27 November 2026). Base text: CBETA Taishō Tripiṭaka.

Because the punctuation is CBETA's and the sectioning is ours, a scholarly citation should name CBETA as the source of the reading and this site only as the place you read it. Where the two differ, CBETA prevails.

Printing and tracing

Printing uses the browser's own dialog. The print stylesheet drops the navigation, the emblem watermark and the toolbar, and leaves a margin for binding; long texts flow across A4 pages without splitting a section heading from its first line. The tracing pages print on A4 as a grid — faint characters to write over, or empty cells for free copying — with no header or footer.

If something is wrong

A missing character, a section boundary in the wrong place, a label that misleads: write to hello@obenno.com or open an issue at github.com/sonefly/obenno/issues. Give the page URL and the section number; for scripture, add the CBETA volume and text number if you have it. Corrections are made on the page once verified, and the date is recorded.

Nothing here requires an account, and nothing is stored on a server: type size, line width and reading position live in your own browser's local storage.
